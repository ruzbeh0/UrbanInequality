# UrbanInequality

UrbanInequality makes building growth reflect socio-economic conditions so your city evolves with realistic inequality patterns instead of upgrading everything at once.

## What the mod does

- Adds education-based progression for residential and commercial upgrades.
- Applies income-driven penalties that slow upgrades in lower-income areas.
- Enforces city-wide building-level caps so only a limited share of buildings can reach top levels.
- Provides wage profiles and city presets so each city can feel different.

## Core mechanics

- **Education-based progression:**  
  Building upgrades are influenced by adult and senior education levels. Higher education speeds up progression.
- **Income-driven penalties:**  
  Low-income households face stricter conditions to level up.
- **City-wide level caps:**  
  You can cap each level’s share of total buildings (for example, only a small percent of all buildings become level 5).
- **Wage profiles:**  
  Resident levels earn configurable monthly wages with presets based on real cities.
- **Building presets:**  
  Choose from city presets (such as Tokyo, São Paulo, Chicago, Santiago, London, and others) to shape inequality outcomes.

## Scope and limitations

- Upgrade restrictions apply to:
  - Residential buildings
  - Commercial buildings
- Wage profiles are not enabled by default; they are selected in settings.
- Existing upgraded buildings are not retroactively downgraded or restricted.
  - New rules apply only to future upgrades after enabling the mod.

## Recommended usage

- Best experience is usually in new cities.
- In existing cities, give it time for upgrade dynamics to adjust with gameplay progression.
